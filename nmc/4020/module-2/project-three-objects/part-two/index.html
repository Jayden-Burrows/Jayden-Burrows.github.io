1. Project Overview & Background

1997 CLASSIC PlayStation Era

The Original Canon

Final Fantasy VII is a landmark Japanese role-playing game (JRPG) released in 1997 by Square (now Square Enix) for the Sony PlayStation. It is widely celebrated for shifting the gaming industry into the 3D era with cinematic cutscenes, pre-rendered backgrounds, and deep tactical combat.

The story begins in the dystopian metropolis of Midgar, ruled by the corrupt Shinra Electric Power Company. Shinra harvests "Mako"—the literal spiritual lifeblood of the planet, known as the Lifestream—to power technology, slowly killing Gaia. Players control Cloud Strife, an ex-SOLDIER mercenary joining AVALANCHE to bomb Shinra's reactors.

During a mission, Cloud meets Aerith Gainsborough, the last surviving Cetra (Ancient). When legendary war hero Sephiroth reappears seeking to absorb the Lifestream and summon Meteor, the party pursues him across the globe. At the story's emotional climax in the Forgotten Capital, Aerith prays for Holy before Sephiroth impales her from above. Her death served as an unalterable narrative anchor that redefined gaming sacrifice, leading the party to ultimately defeat Sephiroth and allow Aerith's spirit in the Lifestream to save the world.

TRILOGY PROJECT 2020 – 2027

The Modern Expansion

Rather than a simple HD remaster, Square Enix re-envisioned the 1997 masterpiece as a multi-part AAA trilogy: Final Fantasy VII Remake (2020), Final Fantasy VII Rebirth (2024), and an upcoming untitled Part 3 (fans call FF7 Revelation).

This modern expansion completely overhauls the original title with photorealistic graphics, fully voiced cutscenes, expanded city districts, and action-hybrid combat. While generally following the primary plot beats of the 1997 release, it weaves in vast amounts of new lore, side quests, and character development.

Crucially, the trilogy introduces a major metaphysical twist: the Whispers (Arbiters of Fate). These spectral entities emerge whenever the party strays from the events of the original 1997 timeline, acting as cosmic guardians who enforce canonical history. When Cloud and his companions destroy the Whisper Harbinger at the Singularity, they shatter the chains of destiny—opening up branching parallel dimensions and alternate timelines (such as one where Cloud's mentor, Zack Fair, survives).

CORE ACADEMIC DEBATE

Remake or Stealth Sequel?

The introduction of Whispers and temporal branches transforms the trilogy from a simple nostalgia trip into an unpredictable multiversal chess match. Both Sephiroth and Aerith appear to possess future knowledge of the original game's tragic outcome—fighting across timeline dimensions to either rewrite or defend the fate of Gaia.

5. Theories & Deep Debate

Nuanced Analysis: Developer Statements & "Advent Children" Connection

It is a common misconception that producer Yoshinori Kitase and director Tetsuya Nomura confirmed the trilogy will strictly lead straight into 2005's Advent Children (AC) unchanged. In interviews, developers stated the story will "connect to Advent Children" or "not betray AC fans," but these statements remain intentionally vague and open to several narrative interpretations:

  • Inevitable Canon: The trilogy is a loop that inevitably resolves into AC regardless of timeline branches.
  • One of Many Worlds: AC represents the bad/depressive outcome of the original timeline that Part 3 aims to break away from.
  • Thematic Link: "Connecting" may refer to emotional continuity and character resolutions rather than a literal 1:1 timeline match.
THEORY 01 Tragic Realism

Canonical Convergence (Aerith Is Dead)

This theory posits that Aerith definitively died at the Forgotten Capital in the primary reality. The alternate worlds shown throughout Rebirth are brief, ephemeral dreams within the Lifestream destined to fade. Cloud's perception of Aerith surviving is a psychological coping mechanism engineered by trauma and amplified by Jenova cells.

Case FOR Theory 1 (Deep Evidence)

  • Jenova Illusion Mechanics: Sephiroth/Jenova feeds on Cloud's mental instability. Cloud seeing a rift in the sky while others mourn mirrors his past denial of Zack's death.
  • The Empty White Materia: Aerith handed Cloud a clear, drained White Materia in her dream world, signifying Holy's power in the primary timeline was preserved only by her physical sacrifice.
  • Thematic Core of Loss: Final Fantasy VII was created as a reflection on irreversible grief. Undoing Aerith's death would undermine the core emotional theme.

Case AGAINST Theory 1

  • Rainbow Particle Effects: Square Enix specifically used rainbow spark effects during Cloud's sword parry—the exact visual effect used when Zack created new timelines.
  • Aerith's Active Interaction: Aerith physically fights alongside Cloud in the void dimension against Sephiroth reborn, showing her agency exceeds a mere illusion.
THEORY 02 Timeline Salvation

Defying Destiny (Aerith Lives in Parallel)

This theory asserts that Cloud successfully altered fate at the moment of impact. The rainbow light confirmed the creation of a timeline branch where Aerith survived Sephiroth's strike. In Part 3, Cloud's task will not be accepting her death, but bridging these worlds to permanently save her and defeat Sephiroth across reality.

Case FOR Theory 2 (Deep Evidence)

  • White vs Black Whispers: The emergence of opposing Whisper factions confirms an active cosmic war over timeline outcomes.
  • Zack's Multiversal Crossover: Zack Fair physically crossed dimensions to assist Cloud and Aerith, establishing a precedent that alternate branch characters can cross over.
  • "7 Seconds Till the End": Sephiroth's quote in Remake refers to the 7 seconds before Aerith's death—offering Cloud the agency to change that exact moment.

Case AGAINST Theory 2

  • World Decay Mechanics: Rebirth shows alternate worlds are unstable and perish quickly. An alternate world where Aerith lives may naturally collapse.
  • Narrative Complexity: Sustaining multiple living versions of characters risks convoluting the ending and alienating mainstream audiences.
