01 Project Overview & Background

When Final Fantasy VII arrived in 1997, it did more than sell well: it pulled Japanese Role-Playing Games into the global mainstream with full 3D worlds, cinematic cutscenes, and a story bold enough to kill off its most beloved heroine, Aerith Gainsborough, halfway through the adventure.

That scene became one of gaming's defining moments, and it is the reason the story still matters to people three decades later. Square Enix leaned on that legacy with a three-part reimagining: Final Fantasy VII Remake (2020), Final Fantasy VII Rebirth (2024), and the finale, Final Fantasy VII Revelation (2027).

The trilogy largely retells the original plot, but it adds major expansions and changes that deliberately unsettle what longtime fans think they know. That unease is exactly where this case study begins.

02 Core Question

Rebirth's ending left the series with one problem that overshadows everything else, so Revelation inherits it before the first cutscene even plays.

Will the main cast successfully change Aerith's fate, or is Cloud hallucinating her survival?

Cloud saw Aerith alive while everyone else saw her fall. Either the party truly rewrote destiny and Aerith lives somewhere, or Cloud, weighed down by Jenova's influence and his fractured memories, is refusing to accept that she is gone. Both readings fit the same scene, and that ambiguity is why we first need to see exactly what the games have shown us.

05 Theory Debate: Fate vs. Convergence

This is the heart of the case study. Everything above, from the original death to Rebirth's split ending and Revelation's promised reckoning, feeds two competing readings. Each is laid out below with an overview, key moments, and links to clips and interviews. Sources are developer interviews and official footage; the interpretation is mine.

Theory A

Canonical Convergence: Aerith Is Dead

Proponents argue that Aerith is physically dead in the primary timeline, exactly as in 1997. The Remake trilogy plays with fate, but it keeps rhyming with the original: the same locations, the same tragic beats, and a developer who has said the Rebirth ending was set early in production and is not a casual fake-out. Under this view, the world where Cloud sees Aerith living is not a rewritten reality but a wound in his mind.

The strongest support is Cloud himself. His memory has never been trustworthy: he mistook another man's life for his own, and Jenova's cells have a history of planting images in his head. If the series wants a satisfying finale, the argument goes, he must face Aerith's death the way the original's Cloud did, and Revelation's central theme of "resolve" and its single ending point in that direction.

Key moments and details

  • Everyone else sees the body. In Rebirth's altar scene the rest of the party watches Aerith fall. Only Cloud perceives her unharmed, which makes him the outlier, not the party.
  • Cloud's unreliable memory. The Nibelheim story, his false identity, and Zack's presence in his mind all show that what Cloud perceives is filtered through trauma and Jenova.
  • The original's structure. Aerith's death drives the Lifestream sequence and the Holy and Meteor finale. Removing the death would pull out the foundation of the whole ending.
  • Mideel is confirmed. The Lifestream breakdown and the rebuilding of Cloud's identity with Tifa are exactly the events a "face the truth" arc needs.
  • Hamaguchi's own framing. He has described Revelation's theme as resolve, promised a single ending, and singled out a scene where Cloud introspects and finds his identity in relation to Zack and Aerith.
  • Aerith as guide. In the original she assists from the Lifestream. The finale can keep that role without her walking among the party again.

But if the series is only repeating history, why did it spend two games breaking the rules of fate?
Theory B

Defying Destiny: Aerith Lives in Parallel

Proponents suggest that the trilogy has been building toward a real break from the original. In Remake the party defeated the Whispers, the entities that enforce fate, and opened branching timelines where Zack survived. In Rebirth, Cloud deflected Sephiroth's blade and split the world, so Aerith survived in one branch while she fell in the other. Under this reading, Cloud's "hallucination" is actually the rare ability to see across the divide.

The argument gains weight from how the finale is staged: a split sky, Zack and Cloud fighting in one realm while Aerith appears in another, and an Aerith who seems to remember events that have not happened. It also fits my own conclusion, because the party can lose her in their world while a parallel Aerith lives on, and the games present two worlds as a mechanic, not a metaphor.

Key moments and details

  • The Whispers are beaten. Remake ends with the guardians of fate destroyed, so the original's script is no longer guaranteed.
  • Zack lives elsewhere. Showing him survive proves the series already uses parallel branches where the original's tragedies are undone.
  • The parried blade. Cloud stops Sephiroth's strike from his own point of view, and the scene splits, which is the clearest "choice" moment in the trilogy.
  • A tear in the sky. As the Highwind leaves, only Cloud notices a rift and a waving Aerith. A rift suggests a place, not just a memory.
  • Aerith knows too much. She acts on knowledge of future events and hands Cloud the white materia across worlds, hinting that she is aware of the multiverse.
  • Deliberate ambiguity. Interviewers have noted the confusing ending may be intentional, and Hamaguchi has said the finale was planned from the start.

Where they clash

Theory A reads Rebirth's split as Cloud's mind; Theory B reads it as the world itself. Nothing the games have shown settles which, and Hamaguchi's "singular ending" means Revelation must pick a lane.

Where they meet

Both agree Cloud must confront something. Whether it is her death or a second world is the one thing the finale will answer, which leads to where I land.

06 Personal Assessment & Conclusion

After weighing both sides, I think the truth sits between them, which also helps explain why the evidence keeps pointing both ways.

My Stance: Aerith may be officially dead within the physical realm of the main cast's world, meaning she will never be able to physically reunite or journey with Cloud, Tifa, and the rest of the main group again. However, through the mechanics of the Lifestream and branching timelines, she will retain the opportunity to live out her life peacefully in a distinct parallel world.
